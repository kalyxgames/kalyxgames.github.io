Break of Dawn - Privacy Policy

Effective: 13 September 2026

Applies to: Break of Dawn and the Break of Dawn Demo, on every platform they are distributed on.

Break of Dawn is made by Benjamin Schultz, trading as Kalyx Games ("we", "us"). This page explains what the game collects, why, where it goes, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read, not skimmed past.

The short version: the game sends anonymous gameplay statistics so we can balance the game and find bugs. It never collects your name, email, Steam account, or anything that identifies you as a person. You can turn it off in Settings.

1. What the game collects

1a. Automatic gameplay telemetry

While you play, the game sends a small snapshot at the end of each wave to a server we operate. Each snapshot contains:

  • Gameplay state: the act and wave, lives remaining, Souls and Sacrifices, which towers are on the board and at what rank, crowns earned, the run's random seed and map layout.
  • Timing: how long the run and the current wave have taken, and the time the snapshot was sent.
  • Performance: frames per second, frame time, and the game speed you were playing at. This is how we find out the game runs badly on some hardware.
  • Game version.
  • A random client ID. A string of random characters generated the first time you run the game and stored in your local settings file. It has no connection to your name, Steam account, hardware, or IP address. Its only purpose is to let us tell that two runs came from the same installation, so a hundred runs from one very dedicated player are not mistaken for a hundred players.
  • A random session ID, new each time you launch the game.

This data is sent to a Cloudflare Worker we control. Like any web server, Cloudflare's infrastructure sees the IP address of the connection while it is being made. We do not store IP addresses and do not link them to gameplay data.

1b. The feedback form

The game has a button that opens a feedback form in your web browser, at kalyxgames.github.io (a page hosted by GitHub Pages). Anything you type there is sent to the same Cloudflare Worker that receives telemetry and stored in a database we control. Nothing in the game is sent to the form automatically; the only thing the game adds is the build version. We ask you not to put personal information in the form beyond what you are comfortable sharing. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to the hosting of the page itself.

1c. Crash reports

If the game closes without shutting down properly, the next time you start it, it asks whether you want to send the previous session's log to us. This is opt-in: nothing is sent unless you choose Send the log. A crash report contains the game's own log messages from that session, the game version, the name and version of your operating system, the name of your graphics card, and the same random client ID as the telemetry. Before sending, the game replaces its data-folder path and your user-folder path with placeholders, so your account name is not included.

2. What the game does not collect

  • Your name, email address, or any contact details
  • Your Steam, itch.io, or any other account information
  • Your IP address (see 1a - it is visible in transit, never stored)
  • Hardware identifiers, MAC addresses, or device serial numbers
  • Your location
  • Anything from outside the game: no files, browsing history, or other applications
  • Voice, camera, or microphone input
  • Payment information (purchases are handled entirely by the store you bought the game on)

The game contains no advertising and no third-party analytics SDK.

3. Why we collect it

We collect gameplay telemetry for two purposes only:

  • Balancing the game. Which towers get built, which waves end runs, how long an act takes. This is how a one-person studio learns whether wave 14 is too hard without watching over a thousand shoulders.
  • Finding bugs and performance problems. A run that ends at 4 frames per second on a particular wave tells us something is wrong there.

Under the GDPR, our legal basis for the automatic telemetry is legitimate interest in improving the game, balanced against the fact that the data is pseudonymous and low-risk. Our basis for the feedback form is your consent, which you give by choosing to send it.

We do not sell this data, share it with advertisers, or use it for anything other than making the game better.

4. Where it goes and who handles it

  • Cloudflare, Inc. operates the Worker that receives telemetry, crash reports and feedback-form submissions, and the database they are stored in. Cloudflare acts as our data processor.
  • GitHub, Inc. hosts the feedback page and this policy (GitHub Pages).

None of these companies are given access to the data for their own purposes. Data may be processed in the United States.

5. How long we keep it

  • Gameplay telemetry is kept for as long as it is useful for balancing the current version of the game, and is deleted or aggregated no later than 24 months after collection.
  • Feedback form responses are kept until we have acted on them.
  • Crash reports are kept until the crash they describe is fixed, and no longer than 24 months.

6. Your choices

  • Crash reports are never automatic. Choose Don't send on the prompt and nothing leaves your computer.
  • Turn off telemetry. Open Settings in the game and switch off "Send anonymous gameplay data". No further snapshots are sent. Snapshots already sent cannot be recalled, because we have no way to tell which ones were yours.
  • Reset your client ID. Delete the game's settings file and a new random ID is generated on the next launch. The old ID cannot be linked back to you by us or anyone else. On Windows the file is at %APPDATA%\Godotpp_userdata\Break of Dawn\settings.cfg.
  • Delete local data. Your run history, seed history and settings are stored in the same folder. Deleting the folder removes everything the game has saved.

Because the telemetry contains no personal identifiers, we cannot look up, correct, or delete "your" records on request - we have no way to know which records are yours. That is by design.

If you are in the EU, UK, or a jurisdiction with similar rights and believe we hold personal data about you, contact us using the details below and we will respond within 30 days.

7. Children

Break of Dawn is not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where that is the local threshold). We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. The game collects no personal data from anyone, so there is no mechanism by which a child's personal data could be collected through it.

8. Steam and other storefronts

If you bought or downloaded the game on Steam, Valve's Steam client collects its own data under Valve's privacy policy, which we do not control. The same applies to itch.io or any other store. This policy covers only what Break of Dawn itself does.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change what the game collects, we will update this page and change the effective date at the top. Changes that expand what is collected will be announced in the game's update notes.
